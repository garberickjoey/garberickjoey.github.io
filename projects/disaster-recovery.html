PROJECT 04 / RESILIENCE

Backup & Disaster Recovery

A practical recovery framework for a small business environment.

RPO

The Recovery Point Objective is the maximum amount of data a business can afford to lose during a disaster

RTO

The Recovery Time Objective is the maximum amount of time a system, application, or business process can remain offline after a disaster.

Testing

Backups are only useful if restores work. Schedule and document restore testing.

Understand that every business is different and a disaster recovery plan is going to look a little different for everybody. The framework below draws from ISO 22301.

Prerequisites

  1. Establish a clear purpose and objectives
  2. Determine Scope (Assets to be protected)
  3. Establish Roles and Responsibilites of team members
  4. Establish Compliance requirements
  5. Note critical services and divide into priority groups
  6. Establish RTO and RPO for priority groups
  7. Define Disaster criteria

Plan Development

  1. For each Disaster criteria in each priority group, outline a basic runbook via a tabletop simulation with stakeholders, from detection to returning to normal operation, noting any possible pain points while doing so.
  2. Take your pain points, and attempt to add compensation. For example, if while performing a tabletop exercise you discover that a if critical business server running on prem is non-recoverable and replacement will be outside of the RTO, it may be prudent to be able to deploy the backup image in a cloud environment until the on prem server can be repaired or replaced.
  3. After compensation, repeat tabletopping and refining until satisfied with the team performance and runbook procedure.

Implementation

  1. Ensure that the team established in the Prerequisites is familiar with the runbooks by running routine simulations and tabletopping.
  2. Establish a periodic audit schedule for established processes.
  3. Document and review every incident to further refine your processes.
